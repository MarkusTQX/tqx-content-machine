If you bought Micron stock every day at market close and sold it at market open, you'd be up 138 million percent.
What if you did the opposite, bought it every day at open and sold it at close?
You'd be down 99 percent.
A researcher at the London School of Economics found this crazy phenomenon happening across hundreds of stocks
where you're statistically most likely to profit if you buy at close and sell at open.
But why?
This is because there's two groups of traders trading at different times.
Individuals trade as soon as the market opens,
while sophisticated hedge funds trade later in the day when there's more liquidity
and they know they can get in at a more profitable price.
In other words, if you're selling at market close,
you might be missing the most profitable window happening overnight.
