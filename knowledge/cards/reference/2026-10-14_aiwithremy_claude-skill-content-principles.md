## Why it works (observations, not guesses)
- The hook is a question the viewer has already asked themselves, and the chat window being stuffed with hook-video thumbnails (1.6–5s) shows the exact habit he's calling out.
- The "why" is concrete and a bit embarrassing: "Step one, make a bold claim. Step two, numbered list. Step three, CTA." (8.7–12.4s). Viewers recognize their own AI output.
- The fix is promised at 15.8s but the name is held back until 25.7s, with the series intro in between, so the loop stays open for 10 seconds.
- A recurring series name ("Skill of the Week", 21.2s) plus an earlier episode's "423K" view count (about 16s) makes it feel established and gives a reason to follow.
- The principles are shown as cards (35–41s), so the viewer gets something even without commenting.
- The result is a screenshot of his own Insights (about 50s), from the same kind of account the viewer runs.
- "I shouldn't even be doing this" (56.5s) right before the keyword adds a sense of insider access to the CTA. 17,827 comments.
- Very fast visual rhythm: a cut about every 2.3s, with a 6-cut burst at 16.6–17.6s right where the fix is introduced.

## Patterns to reuse (structure only)
- "Why is [tool everyone uses] so bad at [job]?" question hook.
- Show the wrong way as a short numbered list on screen, then an empty "why it works?" card to show what's missing.
