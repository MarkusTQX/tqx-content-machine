# Retention writing

Built Oct 23 2026 from 62 videos that did well (`content-machine/knowledge/rules/structure-patterns.md`) and @viewcci's teaching on what comes after the hook. `story-structures` gives the shape; this skill gives the lines that keep people inside it.

## 1. Open a loop, and close it (standard: 42 of 43)
Promise something early that only arrives later. Close every loop you open (`script-logic` #1).

| How | TQX line |
|---|---|
| Announce a count | "There are three checks, and almost nobody runs the third one." |
| Tease the best for last | "The last tool on this list is the one I'd actually use." |
| Promise a result you'll show | "Stick around for the backtest. It's not what you'd expect." |
| Ask a question you answer later | "So why did it lose to doing nothing?" |

## 2. Start, don't finish (standard)
Begin answering right after the hook, but hold the full answer to about 80% (`story-structures`). "If you're gonna answer your hook, first ask another question or delay the solution" (@viewcci).

| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| Hook: "Your backtest is lying to you." Line 2: "Because of overfitting." (done; no reason to stay) | Line 2: "And it's lying in three different ways. The first one's easy to spot." |

## 3. Re-hook around 60–75% (standard: 41 of 62)
One line that pulls attention back before the payoff (`script-logic` #11). **It must carry content.** Stock transitions are banned (`anti-ai-patterns` #4): "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "Here's why that matters".

| Type | What it does | TQX line |
|---|---|---|
| Raise the stakes | Says the worst or best part is still coming | "And that's not even the bad part. Look at the drawdown." |
| Best for last | Names the next item as the important one | "The third check is the one that kills most trading bots." |
| Voice the objection | Says what the viewer is thinking, then answers it | "But wait, you're thinking, 'I can't code.' You don't have to." |
| Remind the promise | Brings the hook back | "Remember that $4,400? Here's where it gets ugly." |
| Ask the why | Turns the next part into an answer | "So why does a bot that made money still lose?" |
| Switch to the screen | Moves from talking to showing | "Let me show you. This is Qube." (with a layout change) |
| Give it away | Raises the value of what's next | "People would pay for this. I'm showing you for free." |

**Raise the stakes without naming what's coming (Mark, Oct 23).** A "craziest part" line that names the next beat ("...is the backtest") gives it away, so there's nothing left to wait for. Mark's version says it's still coming, and the next line delivers it. Same move as his M04 teaser: "And the results were shocking."

| Bad | Fixed (Mark's, J020) |
|---|---|
| "But the craziest part is the backtest." | "That's not even the craziest part." → next line: "Qube takes your strategy and tests it on up to five years of real price data." |
| "That's where things get interesting." (J031 line 9) | "That's when everything changes." (Mark's, J031) |

Real re-hooks from the videos are listed in `structure-patterns.md`. Use them as models, not scripts: write ours in our words (`tqx-voice`).

## 4. Number the middle (common: about 25 of 62)
Announce the count, then say each number out loud: "One… Two… Three…". Each number is a small loop: people stay for the next one. Put the number on screen too (`on-screen-text`).

## 5. Best item last, and say so (option, but strong)
In any list, order from weakest to strongest and flag the last one: "And the most important one…" (M05 put Qube in S tier last; tpl04, tpl44 did the same).

## 6. Voice the viewer's objection (option)
Right when they'd scroll away, say their doubt for them, then answer it. "But coach, how do I…?" / "But wait, you say, I don't have time." In TQX: "But a backtest isn't real money, right? Exactly. That's why paper trading comes next."

## 7. Something changes every 1–3 seconds (standard)
A cut, a new graphic, a number popping in, a punch-in, a layout switch. Plan it line by line (`storyboarding`). The re-hook line gets the biggest visual change.

## 8. Keep it fast (standard)
- Pace: about 3.5 words a second in the videos; ours is 3.0–3.2 (`tqx-voice`). Cut pauses and filler in the edit.
- Credibility in one line, max: "We build AI trading tools for a living."
- No setup sentences that say nothing: "So today I want to talk about…" → cut.
- Specifics hold attention: numbers, names, exact words typed on screen.

## 9. End right after the payoff (standard)
"When you finally reveal the payoff, immediately end the video" (@viewcci). Most videos end within 10 seconds of the payoff, usually just the CTA.
- Payoff → CTA (`cta-and-offer`) → stop. No recap, no "so to sum up".
- Or end on a twist ("So my bot made money, and still lost to doing nothing.") or loop back to the first frame so it replays (option: 5 of 62).

## Checks

- [ ] At least one open loop, and every loop is closed.
- [ ] Line 2 starts answering but doesn't finish.
- [ ] A re-hook at 60–75% that carries content (no stock phrase).
- [ ] Numbered items said out loud and shown, best one last.
- [ ] A visual change planned for every 1–3 seconds.
- [ ] No dead setup lines; credibility is one line at most.
- [ ] The video ends within a few seconds of the payoff.
