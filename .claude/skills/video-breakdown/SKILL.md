# Video breakdown: the lesson card

Mark finds the videos. Your job is to take each one apart the same way, every time, so nothing depends on your own taste. Don't judge whether the topic is "good for TQX" and don't invent a TQX version here; that's `remix-for-product`. Just record what the video does and why it's working.

## Process

1. Get the file (Mark sends it, or download it). Note the link, account, post date, views, likes, comments, shares and saves if visible.
2. Transcribe the audio word for word, with timestamps (Whisper or similar).
3. Grab frames: one every 2 seconds, plus every visible cut. Make a contact sheet.
4. Fill in the card below. Quote, don't paraphrase, for the hook, on-screen text and CTA.
5. Save one card per video, named `{date}_{account}_{short-title}.md`.

## Rules

- One card per video. Never merge two videos into one card. If two links are the same file, say so.
- Record the numbers you can see. Don't estimate views or retention.
- "Why it works" must point to something in the video (a timestamp, a line, a visual), not a general belief.
- Patterns are about structure, pacing and format, never about copying footage or wording.
- Flag overclaims and scam patterns plainly. They're useful as examples of what not to do.

## Card type 2: the teaching card (educational videos)

Use this when the video *teaches* something about making content ("what makes a good hook", "how to structure a Reel"), not when it's a reference video to copy the engine of. These cards feed `kb-curation`, which combines them into `hook-writing`, `story-structures`, `retention-writing` and `storyboarding`.

Same process as above (file, transcript, frames, one card per video), with this card:

```
# [Short title] · @[account] · [platform] · TEACHING
Link: [url]
Posted: [date] · Length: [s] · Views: [n] · Likes: [n] · Comments: [n]
Topic tag: [hooks / storytelling / retention / editing / storyboarding / other]
Feeds skill: [hook-writing / story-structures / retention-writing / storyboarding]
Creator: [who] · Why they might know: [their own results, what they've built, follower count; or "unknown"]

## Rules taught
| # | Their words (quoted) | The rule in one plain line | Example they gave | Evidence they gave | Time |
| 1 | "..." | ... | ... | [data / their own results / none] | 0:12 |

## Examples shown
| Example (exact words, or what's on screen) | Which rule it shows | Time |

## Formulas or frameworks
[Quoted exactly, e.g. a hook formula or a beat structure, with seconds per beat if given]

## Does the video follow its own advice?
[Check its own hook and structure against the rules it teaches: yes / no, with timestamps]

## Conflicts and doubts
- [Rules that clash with other cards or with our skills]
- [Claims with no evidence]
```

Rules for teaching cards:
- One rule per row. If the creator packs two rules into one sentence, split them.
- Quote their words exactly, then write the plain version. Never only paraphrase.
- Write "none" when no evidence is given. Don't upgrade an opinion into a rule.
- Don't write TQX versions here. `kb-curation` and the skill itself do that.

## What we've learned from cards so far (Oct 2026, about 25 videos)

These are observations from the first batch Mark sent. Treat them as single-batch evidence, not rules:
- A comment-keyword CTA appeared in about 13 of 25.
- Common hook openings: "Most people don't realize…", "Claude can now…", "Someone just built…", "Give me 15 seconds…", "explained for 5 year olds".
- Common structure: 3 to 5 numbered steps with an open loop before the last one.
- Trading videos ran 40 to 90 seconds; a UI insert changed every 2 to 3 seconds; captions on every word.
- Formats seen: tier-list ranking, countdown build, "day X of trying...", talking head with a product insert.
- TQX's own data so far: Mark's ranked tool list beat everything else we posted by a wide margin. Videos that give people something they can actually use or compare did better than explainers.
