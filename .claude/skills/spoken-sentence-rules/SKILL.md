# Spoken sentence rules

Every line gets said out loud by Joshua or Mark, in one take, to someone scrolling with the sound on. Write for the ear. American English, the way people in LA actually talk.

The read-aloud test: say the line out loud like you're telling a friend. If you'd stumble, if a friend would say "what?", or if nobody would ever say it that way, rewrite it.

## The rules

### 1. Every sentence ends
A line can't trail off as a fragment that leaves the listener waiting.

| Bad | Fixed (Mark's) |
|---|---|
| "...and test it again. Over and over." | "...and test it again. Over and over until you're happy with the result." |
| "And it stops trading for the day the second you hit your max loss. Even on the days you'd want to keep going." | "And it stops trading for the day the second you hit your max loss, even on the days you'd want to keep going." |

Short punchy lines are fine when they're complete thoughts: "Fewer than 1 in 100." "It meant less."

### 2. Say the actual thing, not a vague noun
"Rules", "it", "they", "this" must point to something the viewer has just heard or can see.

| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "You can ask Claude or Codex to dig through years of past prices and find the rules for you." (What rules?) | "You can open Claude or Codex and type, 'Look at the last 10 years of S&P 500 prices. When should I have bought?' And it'll come back with something like, 'Buy when it drops 2% in a day.'" |
| "Now you hand it to Claude or Codex and they run it version after version." (Hand what? Run what?) | "Now you can type your idea into Claude or Codex, and it'll build it, test it, find what's broken, fix it, and test it again." |

### 3. Use words people actually say
| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "...and keeps to your max loss." | "...and it stops trading for the day the second you hit your max loss." |
| "The bot gets built by typing into Claude." | "So I open Claude, and I just type that out, like I'm texting a friend." |
| "practice account" | "paper trading" / "fake money" |
| "Claude can now build your own trading idea." (Mark: "What the fuck is a trading idea? Does someone from LA walk around saying 'I have a trading idea'?") | "Claude can now build you a trading strategy." / "Tell Claude what you'd buy, and when." |
| "have it test your own idea" | "and it'll test it for you" |
| "...then Qube translates it into implementable code." | "...then Qube translates it into usable code." (Mark, J031) |
| "...stitching together data, charting tools, and backtesting frameworks." | "...putting together data, charting tools, and backtesting frameworks." (Mark, J031) |

### 4. Every line makes sense on its own
Someone might tune in at any line. No line should depend on a word from three lines ago.

| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "And you don't need to know how to code to enter." (Enter what?) | "And you don't need to know how to code to build one." (right after "a team of agents") |
| "That used to take a whole team of people." ("That" is unclear) | "That whole loop used to take a team of people." |

### 5. Be exact about direction and reference points
| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "Sell when it's back up 2%." (Back up from what?) | "Sell once it's up 2% from where I bought, or if it falls another 3%." |
| "...looking for a drop." (A drop in what?) | "...looking for a drop in price." (Mark, J020) |
| "Then an entry rule buys the day one of them drops 3%." (Exactly 3%?) | "Then an entry rule buys the day one of them drops by 3% or more." (Mark, J020) |

### 6. No number out of nowhere
| Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "It made $4,428." | "Say I'd put in $10,000. It made about $4,400 over those ten years." |

### 7. Say numbers the way people say them
- Spoken: round and natural. "about $4,400", "fewer than 1 in 100", "more than 9 out of 10".
- On screen: exact. "+$4,428", "<1%", "93%".

### 8. Explain a term the moment it appears
One plain sentence, right after the term. "That's called a drawdown. It's the biggest drop from a high point before it recovers." (See `terminology` for who needs which terms explained.)

### 9. Shape of a line
- One idea per sentence. One breath per numbered line.
- Mostly 4 to 15 words a sentence. Rarely over 20.
- Talk to "you". The presenter says "I" for his own experience.
- Contractions always: it's, you're, don't, I'd, it'll.
- Starting with "And", "But" or "So" is fine. That's how people talk.

### 10. Punctuation is for delivery
- Periods for pauses. Commas for a short breath.
- No semicolons, no parentheses, no em dashes in spoken lines.
- Quotes only for words someone types or says ("type, 'Build me a team of agents for GATC.'").

### 11. American English
- American spelling: color, analyze, center.
- References that work for US, Hong Kong and Singapore viewers at once. Example: basketball, not American football, because "football" means soccer in HK and SG.
- If a feature is US-only, say "in the US".

### 12. One plain word to make the big moment land
When a line carries the big claim or the worst case, Mark adds one plain emphasis word so it hits harder out loud. One per line, everyday words only, never a buzzword (`anti-ai-patterns` #5).

| Bad | Fixed (Mark's) |
|---|---|
| "I built a quant trading firm on Claude." | "I built an entire quant trading firm on Claude." (M23 hook) |
| "...and even when to get out if it goes wrong." | "...and even when to get out if it all goes wrong." (J022) |
| "For Python from scratch, we have around 300 hours, 200 of those learning to code." | "For Python, we have around 300 hours, 200 of those are just learning to code." (J029) |

## Before handing a script over

Read every line out loud, top to bottom. For each line ask:
1. Does it end?
2. Would a friend know exactly what "it", "they", "rules" or "that" means?
3. Would a guy from LA say it this way?
4. Is every term explained and every number set up?
